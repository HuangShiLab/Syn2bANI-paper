### Shared manuscript claim summary

Syn2bANI uses fixed Type IIB restriction-site tags as positional anchors, chains shared anchors, estimates ANI with a gated likelihood model, and applies ridge calibration for complete-genome real-data comparisons. The same anchors produce aligned fraction, breakpoint, and structural descriptors. The authors support these claims with exact-truth simulations, a GTDB-R207 ANIm benchmark with genome-level train/test separation, calibrated comparator analyses, MAG validation, case studies, and catalog-scale censuses. The manuscript is explicit that calibrated output is regime-specific and that raw gated output is preferred for MAGs and some inconsistent pairs.

## Reviewer 1

### Overall assessment

This is an original and potentially useful method. The restriction-anchor design is well motivated, the exact-truth and real-genome evidence are substantial, and the authors are unusually candid about cases where calibrated FastANI or raw skani outperform their method. The genome-level bootstrap, explicit detection flags, v5/v6 provenance correction, and quality sensitivity analyses are major strengths.

The central hybrid benchmark, however, is not yet clean. The hybrid threshold was selected on the same unified benchmark used for its headline evaluation, which directly weakens the strict holdout claim for the estimator that the manuscript calls its default ANI output. In addition, the benchmark population is conditioned on a skani pre-screen, the calibrated-comparator protocol is not fully equivalent to the Syn2bANI calibration protocol, and the dependence-aware intervals do not cover all principal contrasts. These issues do not erase the evidence that calibrated Syn2bANI improves over the shipped tools on the common held-out set, but they do limit the strength and generality of the comparative conclusions. I would be supportive after independent threshold selection, comparable comparator treatment, complete denominator reporting, and broader uncertainty analyses.

### Who would be interested in the results, and why

Microbiologists, genomic epidemiologists, MAG researchers, and developers of genome-comparison pipelines would be interested in a fast method that reports ANI and structural descriptors in one pass. The results are relevant wherever near-identical ANI values may conceal rearrangements or accessory differences, and the detailed treatment of dependence, calibration leakage, and reliability flags will matter to groups building large-scale GTDB, HROM, or OAPGC workflows.

### Major strengths

1. The benchmark design separates exact truth from real-genome evaluation and uses a large ANIm-truth GTDB set with broad phylum coverage.
2. The calibration and high-ANI splits use bidirectional genome-level exclusion, and the v5/v6 labeling correction is documented without changing numerical values.
3. The authors disclose inconvenient results, including better performance by raw skani above 90% ANI and better performance by calibrated FastANI after post-hoc correction.
4. The GTDB uncertainty audit resamples whole genomes rather than treating pairs as independent, and the census analyses clearly state the limits of species-level resampling.
5. Detection-floor behavior, consistency flags, and one-sided upper bounds are reported rather than hidden.

### Major Concerns

R1-M1 [leakage-control]  
**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** Yes  
**Claim pointer** The hybrid estimator, with a raw-ANI threshold of 98%, is presented as the default output and is evaluated on the unified 80-100% GTDB benchmark.  
**Evidence pointer** Manuscript lines 59, 79, and 399-420; Supplementary Note 6 lines 559-580; `scripts/generate_fig3_gtdb_benchmark.py` lines 30-74 and 183-193.  
**Concern** Supplementary Note 6 states that the hybrid threshold was chosen by overall MAE on the unified benchmark containing the 43,334 held-out pairs plus the 727 high-ANI test pairs. Figure 3 and Table 1 then report performance of that rule on the same data. This is test-set model selection. The flat plateau between 97% and 99% reduces practical concern but does not provide an unbiased evaluation of the selected rule. The strict holdout language is appropriate for v6 calibration but should not be extended to the hybrid estimator.  
**Why it matters** The hybrid output is defined in Table 1 as the manuscript's default ANI and is the headline method in Figure 3. If its operating rule was tuned on the reported benchmark, the central comparative result is partly in-sample and cannot establish independent performance.  
**Resolution test** Select the threshold using only training or nested-validation folds, freeze it, and evaluate once on an untouched genome-disjoint benchmark. Alternatively, pre-specify a threshold from scientific or deployment criteria and report the current analysis as a sensitivity study rather than confirmatory evaluation. The refrozen rule and all model-selection steps should be auditable.

R1-M2 [experimental-design]  
**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** No  
**Claim pointer** The GTDB benchmark measures broad accuracy of Syn2bANI, skani, and FastANI on representative same-genus pairs.  
**Evidence pointer** Manuscript lines 254-260 and 399-420; `results/gtdb50k/GTDB50K_HELDOUT_REPORT.md` lines 35-58 and 117-128; Supplementary Note 6 lines 563-565.  
**Concern** The 43,334-pair holdout was sampled from pairs passing a skani pre-screen of AF at least 15% and was stratified using skani-defined ANI bands. The unified hybrid analysis has calibrated values for 40,629 of the 44,061 potential pairs, while the figure legend describes the full 43,334 plus 727 set and the comparator rows cover all calls. Thus the target population is conditioned on skani output, and the methods do not necessarily share denominators. The report provides useful common-subset rows, but the headline comparison does not consistently carry that qualification.  
**Why it matters** The estimand changes when pairs are selected by one comparator and when one method declines calibrated output. Differential exclusion can especially affect the 80-85% band, where Syn2bANI is weakest and 3,431 pairs are below detection.  
**Resolution test** Define whether the intended population is skani-screenable representative pairs or all same-genus pairs. For the intended population, report a single prespecified denominator for every method, operational fallback rules for flagged pairs, all-pair and common-pair analyses, and a sensitivity analysis using an independent or unconditioned sampling frame.

R1-M3 [comparator-fairness]  
**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** No  
**Claim pointer** Comparators are evaluated as shipped and are then given an equivalent post-hoc calibration to determine whether Syn2bANI remains superior.  
**Evidence pointer** Manuscript lines 57-62, 190-209, and 278; Table 1 lines 401-420; `results/gtdb50k/CALIBRATED_COMPARATORS.md`.  
**Concern** The shipped comparison reports skani defaults even though the manuscript states that skani documentation recommends `-c` adjustments below 90% ANI. The calibrated-comparator analysis is then described as band-holdout on the held-out benchmark, whereas Syn2bANI v6 was trained on separate genome-disjoint data. FastANI is also allowed two additional mapped-fragment features. These choices answer different questions, but they are not a single best-practice comparison under identical tuning and feature budgets. The upper-panel FastANI row is additionally labelled as a 363-pair subset without a full failure-handling policy.  
**Why it matters** Readers cannot tell whether the relevant conclusion concerns defaults, tuned tools, or an idealized post-hoc ensemble. The current framing is transparent, but the word “same” in the comparator-calibration context could imply closer equivalence than the supplied evidence establishes.  
**Resolution test** Report separate, clearly labelled tiers for shipped defaults, vendor-recommended settings, and calibrators trained under identical external folds, feature budgets, and failure rules. For tools with non-calls, define whether non-calls are failures, exclusions, or imputed values before computing headline metrics.

R1-M4 [calibration-provenance]  
**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** No  
**Claim pointer** Benchmark calibration v6 is genome-disjoint from the held-out benchmark and is suitable for complete-genome comparisons.  
**Evidence pointer** `results/gtdb50k/CALIBRATION_PROVENANCE.md`; manuscript lines 190-209 and 254-259; Supplementary Note 6 lines 512-557 and Supplementary Note 2 lines 401-414.  
**Concern** Genome-level disjointness is necessary but may not be sufficient. Training and evaluation come from the same GTDB release and overlapping taxonomic space, while the packet does not quantify genus, family, or phylum overlap between training and test genomes. The high-ANI expansion was selected by skani screening, and v6 differs from the v5 model embedded in the software snapshot. The supplied provenance lists model files but not checksums, exact pair manifests, frozen software commits, or environment versions. The learning curve is for v5 only.  
**Why it matters** These gaps leave open whether v6 learns general ANI-calibration relationships or release-specific, screen-conditioned, and clade-correlated structure. The distinction between deployed and benchmark-only models also affects reproducibility of the published numbers.  
**Resolution test** Release immutable manifests and checksums for all training, validation, and test pairs; quantify taxonomic overlap; perform genus- or family-block cross-validation in addition to band holdout; and provide v6 learning curves. An external validation set from another release or another complete-genome resource would materially strengthen the calibration claim. The manuscript should state unambiguously whether v6 is intended for deployment.

R1-M5 [statistical-methods]  
**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** No  
**Claim pointer** The uncertainty analysis accounts for the dominant dependence in the benchmark and supports comparative conclusions.  
**Evidence pointer** `results/revision/DEPENDENCE_AWARE_UNCERTAINTY_REPORT.md`; `results/revision/dependence_aware_uncertainty.tsv`; `scripts/revision/dependence_aware_uncertainty.py` lines 26-89 and 157-176; manuscript lines 123, 133, 227, 229, and 232.  
**Concern** The genome-level bootstrap is appropriate for pair overlap but is supplied only for Syn2bANI-v6 minus skani and hybrid minus skani on the common calibrated subset. There is no corresponding interval for the FastANI contrast, absolute MAEs, band-specific contrasts, or phylum-level summaries. The genome bootstrap handles resampled genomes but does not model shared ancestry or the sampling structure of GTDB. Species-level bootstrap intervals for HROM and OAPGC quantify species composition while the authors explicitly note that they do not remove pair-level dependence, shared ancestry, or quality correlation. GTDB census estimates are descriptive and receive no interval.  
**Why it matters** Large point differences make the main trend plausible, but the current uncertainty map does not align one-to-one with all comparative and band-specific claims. This is especially important because methods rankings change across ANI bands.  
**Resolution test** Predefine primary contrasts and report genome- or clade-aware intervals for each, including FastANI and the principal band-level claims. For censuses, either use hierarchical resampling or mixed models that address within-species and ancestry structure or retain the current estimates as explicitly descriptive. Release seeds and replicate-level summaries where feasible.

R1-M6 [ground-truth-validity]  
**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** No  
**Claim pointer** dnadiff-derived ANIm is treated as the real-genome truth across the main benchmark.  
**Evidence pointer** Manuscript lines 190-195, 246-250, and 255-259; `results/gtdb50k/GTDB50K_HELDOUT_REPORT.md` lines 117-128; Supplementary Figure S4 lines 41-54.  
**Concern** The holdout includes 20,639 low-AF and 59 very-low-AF truth pairs, in addition to 22,636 strict-AF pairs. The supplied report gives AF-tier counts but not ANI accuracy stratified by truth AF. This matters because ridge calibration receives query and reference aligned fractions as features and the likelihood deliberately excludes accessory content. At low shared content, ANIm may summarize conserved aligned regions rather than whole-genome identity in the same sense intended by all estimators.  
**Why it matters** Calibration could partly learn the truth pipeline's alignment behavior rather than biological divergence, and comparator rankings may differ when alignment coverage is poor. The issue does not invalidate ANIm as a standard reference, but its domain of reliability needs to be characterized.  
**Resolution test** Report overall, per-band, and comparator-contrast metrics by strict, low, and very-low truth-AF strata. Add sensitivity analyses excluding very-low-AF pairs, varying the minimum required alignment coverage, or using an independent aligner for a representative subset.

### Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established

The blocking issue is R1-M1. The hybrid rule used for the headline benchmark requires threshold selection outside the reported test set and a new frozen evaluation. R1-M2 through R1-M6 should also be resolved or used to narrow the claims, but they do not by themselves negate the evidence for calibrated Syn2bANI on the common held-out subset.

### Assessment against Nature-style criteria

**Originality** Strong. Fixed restriction-site anchors used simultaneously for chained ANI estimation and structural descriptors are a distinctive contribution relative to conventional k-mer ANI tools.

**Scientific importance** High if the comparative claims are narrowed appropriately. The demonstration that near-identical ANI can coexist with structural differences is valuable across microbial genomics, MAG analysis, and surveillance.

**Interdisciplinary readership** Good. The problem is broadly relevant, and the introduction explains the motivation clearly. The calibration, benchmark, and dependence details are specialized but necessary for the methods audience.

**Technical soundness** Substantial but not yet fully established for the hybrid headline. The raw and v6-calibrated estimates are supported by careful simulation and genome-disjoint GTDB evidence. The test-set-selected hybrid threshold, differential denominators, comparator protocol differences, and incomplete uncertainty coverage require correction or claim narrowing.

**Readability for nonspecialists** Generally clear for the main concept, especially through the exact-truth examples and structural case studies. Benchmark variants, model versions, and analysis-specific denominators are dense and should be consolidated into a concise decision guide.

### Recommendation posture

Promising and close to convincing for the calibrated complete-genome estimator, but currently not established from the provided evidence as an independently evaluated hybrid default. Supportive if the hybrid selection, comparator-fairness, calibration provenance, denominator, and uncertainty issues are resolved.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- The claim that the hybrid rule recovers high-ANI performance on the unified benchmark is not independently established because the threshold was tuned on that benchmark.
- The manuscript supports a performance advantage over shipped skani on the common calibrated subset, but broader superiority claims should be limited until comparator tuning, denominators, and FastANI uncertainty are handled comparably.
- The claim of genome-level leakage control is well supported for v6 calibration, but broader calibration generalization across clades and GTDB releases is not yet demonstrated in the packet.
- The low-AF robustness of ANIm-truth comparisons is not demonstrated by the supplied benchmark report.
- I did not visually inspect rendered figures and did not recompute results from the underlying pair-level matrices, so visual accessibility and exact numerical reproduction remain outside the present assessment boundary.
