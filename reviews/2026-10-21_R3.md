# Mock review, Reviewer 3

## Review setup

### Input scope

This review is based only on `reviews/NATURE_REVIEW_PACKET_2026-10-21.md`, the primary manuscript, the Supplementary Information, the listed benchmark, census, uncertainty and QC reports, the listed tabular outputs, and the four listed figure or analysis scripts. I did not read `reviews/2026-10-21_R1.md` or `reviews/2026-10-21_R2.md`. I did not run the software or perform new analyses. Figure consistency was assessed from the manuscript legends, supplementary captions, tables, source reports and supplied scripts, not from visual inspection of rendered image files.

### Assessment boundary

The review assesses the evidence supplied in the packet. Claims in external repositories are treated as described by the manuscript unless a source report in the packet substantiates them. Where reproducibility depends on a file, command, version or metadata item not included in the packet, the concern is identified as not assessable from the supplied material rather than inferred.

### Shared manuscript claim summary

The authors present Syn2bANI as a method that digests genomes in silico with Type IIB enzymes, chains shared positional anchors, estimates ANI with a chain-restricted maximum-likelihood model, and reports aligned fraction and structural descriptors in the same comparison. The central performance claims are high accuracy in exact-truth simulations, improved ANI agreement with ANIm after real-genome calibration, correct regime-specific use of raw and calibrated outputs, and useful structural localization that scalar ANI cannot provide. The authors then apply structural and ANI layers to isolate collections, H. pylori loci, HROM, OAPGC and GTDB. The census analyses are appropriately described as hybrid analyses using the companion Syn2b structural channel and skani ANI rather than end-to-end Syn2bANI runs.

### Visible evidence base

The strongest evidence in the packet includes exact-truth simulations, a 2,074-pair calibration benchmark, a 43,334-pair GTDB held-out benchmark with 39,903 pairs having calibrated values, calibrated comparator analyses, CAMI2 MAG tests, structural validations against dnadiff or minimap2, and census QC and sensitivity tables. The calibration provenance report clearly distinguishes deployed v5 from benchmark v6 and explains the genome-level leakage controls. The dependence-aware uncertainty audit supplies genome-level and species-level bootstrap intervals. The HROM, OAPGC and GTDB reports provide counts, quality strata and audit results that mostly reconcile with the manuscript.

### Missing materials affecting confidence

The supplied packet does not include a single executable reproduction artifact for all reported analyses, the complete pair-level census tables, the full command manifest for every figure, or all source files referenced in the data availability statement. It also does not provide the operational rules used to classify minimap2-supported structural events as genuine beyond the summary descriptions. These gaps directly affect the reproducibility and data-availability assessment below.

## Reviewer 3

### Overall assessment

This is an ambitious and unusually transparent methods manuscript. The anchoring idea is clearly motivated, the likelihood and calibration logic are documented in detail, and the authors are commendably candid about negative results, prior bugs, regime-specific failure and the boundary between Syn2bANI and companion-tool analyses. The GTDB calibration provenance and dependence-aware uncertainty audits are particularly valuable.

The methodological case is promising but not yet fully reproducible from the supplied material. The released software description, benchmark model and structural rerun version are not unified in the code availability statement. The main unified benchmark and Figure 3 use a complete-case hybrid subset that is not stated consistently in the headline claims. Efficiency results contain mutually inconsistent timing, memory and task descriptions. The census analyses are well summarized, but the pair-level data are promised only upon acceptance. Finally, structural validation language is broader than the heterogeneous metric-specific evidence and some matching rules are not fully specified. These issues are addressable, but they are important for a methods audience and for nonspecialist readers who need to know exactly which output to use.

### Who would be interested in the results, and why

Developers of genome-comparison tools would be interested in the use of sparse positional anchors to combine ANI estimation with aligned fraction and structural descriptors. Microbiologists and epidemiologists studying closely related isolates would be interested in the demonstration that high ANI does not necessarily imply conserved architecture. Researchers working with MAGs, oral-airway catalogs or large reference databases may value the regime-specific guidance and census-scale sensitivity analyses. The manuscript is also relevant to groups that currently use FastANI or skani and need to understand where calibrated and uncalibrated methods differ.

### Major strengths

1. The central method is conceptually coherent. The manuscript explains why recognition-site loss and tag-body mismatches carry different information, why chaining separates shared-content estimation from divergence estimation, and why coordinate-carrying anchors can produce structural summaries (manuscript lines 39-45 and 151-180).

2. The benchmark design and model provenance are largely explicit. The manuscript distinguishes deployed v5 from benchmark v6, reports the 2,520-pair and 4,134-pair training sets, and describes bidirectional genome-level exclusion (manuscript lines 53-61 and 190-209; `results/gtdb50k/CALIBRATION_PROVENANCE.md`).

3. The paper does not hide comparator sensitivity. It reports that calibration gives FastANI 0.548 MAE versus 0.619 for calibrated Syn2bANI on the common 39,903 pairs and states that the headline advantage over tools as shipped mostly reflects removal of comparator bias (manuscript lines 61; `results/gtdb50k/CALIBRATED_COMPARATORS.md`).

4. The dependence-aware uncertainty analysis matches the structure of the data better than a naive pair bootstrap. Whole-genome resampling is used for the GTDB benchmark and species-level resampling for HROM and OAPGC, with explicit limits (manuscript lines 59 and 227-229; `results/revision/DEPENDENCE_AWARE_UNCERTAINTY_REPORT.md`).

5. The structural section is unusually candid about a serious historical bug. It documents inflated self-comparison breakpoints, withdrawal of earlier values, the corrected definition and the resulting validation (manuscript lines 85-93 and 234-240).

### Major Concerns

R3-M1 [methodological reproducibility]

**Severity** Major

**Blocking** Yes

**Claim pointer** The software, models and analyses are freely available and reproducible.

**Evidence pointer** Code availability, manuscript lines 290-294; Table 1, lines 399-420; `results/gtdb50k/CALIBRATION_PROVENANCE.md`.

**Concern** The available-code narrative does not map cleanly to the evaluated artifacts. The released Syn2bANI snapshot is stated to be version 0.1.0 with embedded calibration v5 and commit `fe0f36c`, while the main real-genome benchmark uses calibration v6 and several structural case studies are described as reruns with Syn2bANI v0.1.1. A key structural metric also depends on companion Syn2b, for which no version or commit is supplied. The calibration report correctly resolves stale v5 labels, but a reader still cannot assemble one versioned software stack corresponding to all reported results from the manuscript alone. The 120 passing tests are asserted without an environment lock, container identifier or continuous-integration artifact in the supplied material.

**Why it matters** Reproducibility is central for a methods paper. The distinction between v5 and v6 is scientifically important because it changes benchmark conclusions, and the distinction between v0.1.0 and v0.1.1 affects the corrected breakpoint statistic. Without exact executable versions, model hashes and analysis commands, independent users may reproduce a different method while believing they have reproduced the paper.

**Resolution test** Release immutable tagged artifacts for Syn2bANI 0.1.0 and 0.1.1, the companion Syn2b version used for every dependent analysis, the v5 and v6 model files with checksums, environment or container definitions, and a top-level run manifest that maps each table and figure to the exact software, model, inputs and commands. State explicitly which release reproduces each main claim.

R3-M2 [claim-to-evidence mapping]

**Severity** Major

**Blocking** No

**Claim pointer** The unified GTDB benchmark covers 43,334 held-out pairs plus 727 high-ANI test pairs.

**Evidence pointer** Abstract, manuscript lines 17 and 59; Figure 3 legend, line 370; `scripts/generate_fig3_gtdb_benchmark.py`; Supplementary Note 6, lines 559-581.

**Concern** The headline unified analysis is a complete-case analysis but is often described by its source cohorts rather than its denominator. The strict held-out set contains 43,334 pairs, of which 39,903 have calibrated values. The combined source set is 44,061 pairs, while Supplementary Note 6 states that the hybrid threshold table uses 40,629 pairs with non-missing raw and calibrated values. The Figure 3 script returns a missing hybrid value whenever either raw or calibrated value is missing, so missing calibrated values also remove a pair from the plotted hybrid analysis even if the raw value exists. The abstract also calls the 39,903 calibrated pairs the held-out comparison without stating that this is a subset of the 43,334-pair holdout.

**Why it matters** A reader cannot tell from the headline alone whether 0.615 MAE is computed on 44,061, 43,334, 40,629 or another subset. Because `BELOW_DETECTION` pairs are concentrated at low ANI, the denominator may also affect apparent per-band performance and the interpretation of the detection boundary.

**Resolution test** Report the number of usable pairs for every method, band and panel next to each metric. In the abstract and Figure 3 legend, state that the unified hybrid metric is complete-case for non-missing raw and calibrated values. Add a sensitivity analysis that assigns an explicit rule, such as the raw gated estimate or a flagged value, to pairs lacking calibrated output.

R3-M3 [figure and table consistency]

**Severity** Major

**Blocking** No

**Claim pointer** Syn2bANI has favorable runtime and memory relative to alignment-based and two-tool workflows.

**Evidence pointer** Results, manuscript line 63; Table 2, lines 422-438; Figure 4 legend, lines 372; Supplementary Table S6, lines 318-355.

**Concern** The efficiency evidence is not internally consistent. Results describe 3.7 s from FASTA and 2.8 s with sketch reuse as ANI-only comparison, followed by ANI plus SV in 7.6 s, whereas Table 2 labels both the 3.7 s and 2.8 s rows as ANI plus SV in one pass. Results report 58 MB peak memory with sketch reuse, while Table 2 reports 0.012 GB, or 12 MB, for the corresponding sketch-reuse row. Results state that the 5,000-genome triangle takes about 14 min, whereas Supplementary Table S6 reports 406.7 s, or about 6.8 min, for n = 5,000. Results say `syn2bani struct` uses at most 15 MB, while Table 2 reports 0.000038 GB, or about 0.038 MB. The 484 comparisons for 22 genomes also require clarification because 22 genomes yield 231 unordered non-self pairs or 462 ordered non-self pairs, not 484, unless self comparisons are included.

**Why it matters** These discrepancies affect a headline practical advantage and make it difficult to know which task, denominator and hardware produced each number. They also reduce confidence in reuse of the timing table by future users.

**Resolution test** Reconcile every timing and memory value with a machine-readable table that distinguishes ANI-only, ANI plus SV, SV-only, FASTA mode, sketch mode, cold index load and warm queries. State whether self comparisons are included, report the exact pair count and hardware configuration, and round only after the final task definitions are fixed.

R3-M4 [data and code availability]

**Severity** Major

**Blocking** Yes

**Claim pointer** All benchmark datasets, ground-truth files, evaluation matrices and figure-generation scripts are available.

**Evidence pointer** Data availability, manuscript lines 286-294; census methods, lines 222-232; `results/census/gtdb/GTDB_ANI_MERGE_QC_NOTES.md`; `results/census/hrom/quality_stratified/QUALITY_STRATIFIED_REPORT.md`.

**Concern** The statement says that pair-level census tables will be deposited in Zenodo upon acceptance. The HROM, OAPGC and GTDB censuses are major results, and the supplied packet contains species-level summaries and QC tables but not the pair-level records needed to reproduce joins, exclusions, filters and species assignments independently. The manuscript also states that the H. pylori cagPAI analysis uses a separate collaborator dataset for cagA, vacA and CARD metadata, but the supplied materials do not describe access conditions, governance or a versioned distribution path for those metadata beyond case-study result paths. Other database inputs and scripts are asserted to be in repositories that are not fully inspectable from the packet.

**Why it matters** The census conclusions depend on canonical pair construction, exact `C(n,2)` coverage, missing-value treatment and metadata joins. Availability only after acceptance prevents full review-stage verification of these steps. Collaborator metadata require a clear access statement so that users know whether the exact analysis set can be reused and under what restrictions.

**Resolution test** Deposit an immutable review-stage archive containing pair-level census outputs or a documented reduced but sufficient verification dataset, checksums, source accession manifests, filtering code and provenance metadata. For restricted collaborator data, provide a controlled-access route or explicit statement that the data cannot be redistributed, together with code and synthetic or aggregate checks sufficient to audit the join. Include the final accepted-data plan and any privacy or governance constraints.

R3-M5 [technical soundness]

**Severity** Major

**Blocking** Yes

**Claim pointer** The reported structural outputs agree with independent alignment-based truth.

**Evidence pointer** Structural validation, manuscript lines 89-97; SV methods, lines 234-240; Figure 5 legend, line 374; Supplementary Table S7, lines 357-383.

**Concern** The evidence is much stronger for `raw_inverted_fraction` than for all structural outputs. The manuscript reports Pearson r = 0.936 for the companion-tool inverted fraction on the held-out set and r = 0.996 in the high-ANI set, but `breakpoint_count` has partial r = 0.411 and raw r = 0.361 after correction, while `af_query` has Spearman r = 0.39 against dnadiff and 0.26 against minimap2 coverage. Calling all of this agreement in Figure 5 and saying that `af_query` is confirmed by these correlations overstates the weakest result. In addition, the SV method says validation used span-based one-to-many matching but does not define the span-overlap threshold, event-classification rules, resolution tolerance or decision procedure for calling minimap2-supported events genuine. The FDA-ARGOS table provides counts but not an auditable classification rule.

**Why it matters** Different structural descriptors have different failure modes and different levels of validation. A nonspecialist reader may interpret the section as showing that breakpoint counts, chain coverage and inversion fractions are all near-alignment accuracy. Reproducibility also requires exact matching criteria, especially because the authors themselves show that circular-origin artifacts and assembly fragmentation can create apparent events.

**Resolution test** Separate validation claims by metric and report effect size, absolute error, confidence interval, event-size range and failure modes for each. Define the one-to-many matching rule, overlap or distance thresholds, artifact handling, minimap2 classification workflow and inter-run or inter-reviewer quality control. Reword captions so high agreement is claimed only for the metrics that support it.

R3-M6 [readability for nonspecialists]

**Severity** Major

**Blocking** No

**Claim pointer** Users can select the appropriate raw, calibrated or hybrid output for complete genomes, drafts, MAGs and mixed inputs.

**Evidence pointer** Output-selection guidance, manuscript lines 73-80; Table 1 note, line 399; Supplementary Note 1, lines 388-399; flag definitions in lines 165-180 and Supplementary Note 4, lines 429-487.

**Concern** The regime-specific guidance is honest but difficult for nonspecialists to operationalize. The raw gated estimate is preferred for MAGs and mid-ANI pairs, calibration is intended for complete-genome comparisons, the hybrid rule uses raw at 98% or above and calibration below that threshold, flags can alter interpretation, and Table 1 says that unlabeled ANI means hybrid default output. A reader encounters several output names and model versions before seeing a single concise decision table. The units are also mixed, with the abstract using percentage error and much of the text using ANI points.

**Why it matters** Incorrect output selection is not cosmetic. The authors show that applying v5 to MAGs raises MAE from 0.163 to 1.261 and can push near-clonal pairs above 100. Clear output semantics are therefore part of correct use rather than a presentation preference.

**Resolution test** Add an early decision matrix with columns for input type, ANI regime, recommended output, meaning of flags and whether calibration should be displayed or suppressed. Use one unit, preferably ANI percentage points, consistently from the abstract through tables. Define the software default and ensure the command-line output identifies the model and rule that produced every column.

R3-M7 [claim-to-evidence mapping]

**Severity** Major

**Blocking** No

**Claim pointer** Syn2bANI remained robust to indels, accessory content and fragmentation.

**Evidence pointer** Simulation results, manuscript lines 47-51; simulation framework, lines 242-244.

**Concern** The exact-truth experiments are well constructed, but the broad word robust outpaces the sampled simulation space. The main ANI, indel, fragmentation and accessory sweeps derive from one E. coli reference. Substitutions are uniform across alternative bases, with no GC-biased mutation model, transition-transversion bias, codon structure or selection. GC coverage uses five templates but the main robustness claim is broader than these conditions. This is not a challenge to the simulated results themselves, which are transparently specified.

**Why it matters** Robustness in simulation is template-specific and model-specific. A reader may extrapolate performance to genomes or evolutionary regimes that were not represented, especially because real GTDB data reveal a large raw bias that simulations alone did not predict.

**Resolution test** Qualify the robustness claim by simulation family, reference background and substitution model. If broader robustness is intended, it should be supported by additional diverse templates and mutational processes, but claim adjustment alone would resolve the current mismatch between evidence and wording.

### Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established

Blocking concerns R3-M1, R3-M4 and R3-M5 must be resolved. In short, the exact evaluated software and data artifacts must be reproducible, review-stage access to census and metadata provenance must be sufficient, and structural-output validation must be metric-specific and operationally defined. R3-M2 and R3-M3 additionally require correction before the numerical headline and efficiency claims should be treated as settled.

### Assessment against Nature-style criteria

**Originality.** The use of fixed Type IIB anchors for joint ANI and structural reporting is an interesting alternative to scalar k-mer comparison. The manuscript distinguishes Syn2bANI from 2bRAD-M and from FastANI or skani, but the supplied packet does not support a comprehensive novelty audit beyond the cited framing.

**Scientific importance.** ANI is widely used, and the demonstration that very high ANI can conceal structural and resistance-profile differences is potentially important. The large benchmarks and catalog analyses increase practical relevance.

**Interdisciplinary readership.** Microbiologists, computational biologists and public-health researchers should understand the motivation. However, the many output modes, model versions, companion-tool boundaries and census layers currently require assembly by the reader. A decision matrix and simplified throughline would improve accessibility.

**Technical soundness.** The likelihood, calibration, leakage controls, uncertainty audits and negative controls are well documented. The main technical weakness is not absence of evidence but incomplete versioning, complete-case reporting, internal numerical consistency and operational definitions for some structural validation.

**Readability for nonspecialists.** The opening and Fig. 1 concept are helpful. The results become dense because accuracy, runtime, structural validation, pathogenicity islands and three censuses are packed into one narrative. Metric-specific language and clearer output-selection guidance are needed.

### Recommendation posture

Promising and potentially valuable, but currently not fully established as a reproducible Nature Methods methods package. I would be supportive after the versioned software and data artifacts, complete-case reporting, numerical reconciliation and metric-specific structural validation are addressed. The core concept does not require reinvention, but the present manuscript needs stronger reproducibility and claim-to-evidence alignment.

## Risk / unsupported claims

- Novelty. The manuscript does not use an explicit first-to-do claim. Its novelty case rests on the supplied contrast with 2bRAD-M and k-mer tools. A broader prior-tool comparison is not assessable from the packet.
- Accuracy significance. The 0.619 versus 0.957 and 0.977 MAE comparison as shipped is supported on the common 39,903-pair subset. The broader significance is limited by the calibrated FastANI result of 0.548 and the authors own statement that the shipped-tool gain mostly reflects bias removal.
- Unified-benchmark significance. The 0.615 hybrid result is supported as a complete-case value in the supplied threshold analysis, but the manuscript headline does not consistently state that denominator.
- Efficiency significance. Runtime and memory advantages are plausible but not currently established from the packet because the relevant values differ across Results, Table 2 and Supplementary Table S6.
- Structural generalization. Evidence supports collection-specific detection of structural differences and strong agreement for the companion-tool inversion fraction. It does not yet support an unqualified statement that all structural outputs agree with alignment truth.
- Census inference. HROM and OAPGC are explicitly described as candidate divergence-mode or association screens rather than direct mutation-rate or migration measurements. That caution should be retained. GTDB point estimates are appropriately treated as observational catalog summaries, but review-stage verification depends on data not yet supplied.
- Readability assessment limitation. I could not visually inspect the rendered figure files because they were not included in the listed packet. Figure findings therefore rely on legends, captions, tables, reports and supplied plotting code.

Report frozen 2026-10-21.
