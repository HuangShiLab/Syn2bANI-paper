# Reviewer 2 report, Syn2bANI manuscript

## Review setup

### Input scope

This review is based on the packet dated 21 October 2026 and only the files it
lists. The primary materials are `paper/manuscript/manuscript_nature_methods.md`
and `paper/manuscript/SUPPLEMENTARY.md`. I also consulted the supplied benchmark
provenance and reports, uncertainty audit, census summaries and QC notes, census
sensitivity tables, and figure scripts. I did not read `reviews/2026-10-21_R1.md`
or `reviews/2026-10-21_R3.md`.

### Assessment boundary

The assessment is bounded by the supplied material. I did not perform external
literature searching, rerun the software, inspect rendered figures, or evaluate
claims about novelty relative to work outside the packet. Pair-level HROM and
GTDB census tables were not among the review inputs, so some census concerns are
assessable only at the species or stratum level. Missing locations are identified
rather than inferred.

### Shared manuscript claim summary

Syn2bANI uses Type IIB restriction-site tags as positional anchors, estimates ANI
from tags in trusted collinear chains, and reports aligned fraction and
structural descriptors in the same pass. The manuscript supports the estimator
with exact-truth simulations, an ANIm-referenced GTDB benchmark, MAG tests, and
case studies. It then presents HROM, OAPGC and GTDB within-species censuses. The
censuses are explicitly described as hybrid analyses in which structural features
come from the companion Syn2b structural channel and ANI comes from skani.

### Visible evidence base

The strongest evidence for the core estimator includes exact-truth simulation
results, the 39,903-pair GTDB held-out comparison against ANIm, the genome-level
bootstrap audit, MAG quality stratification, explicit comparator recalibration,
and structural controls. For the census, the supplied evidence includes
22,536 species rows in the GTDB summary and joint tables, 1,311 complete species
with at least 100 expected pairs in Figure 11, HROM mode counts and quality
stratification, and 200 OAPGC species tests. The OAPGC median sequence-distance
AUC is 0.574 (95% CI 0.555 to 0.582) and the median breakpoint AUC is 0.494
(95% CI 0.489 to 0.509). The GTDB all-pairs breakpoint-positive fraction is
77.93% among reported pairs and 86.66% among breakpoint-informative pairs.

### Missing materials affecting confidence

Pair-level census records, ancestry or phylogenetic distance tables, site and
sample metadata, and rendered census figures were not in the supplied packet.
The companion Syn2b version used for the census structural layer is also not
stated with the precision given for Syn2bANI and skani. These gaps do not prevent
a review of the manuscript's logic, but they limit verification of whether the
proposed census labels survive joint modeling at the pair and lineage levels.

## Reviewer 2

### Overall assessment

The manuscript is unusually candid about several limits of ANI and of its own
structural statistics. The high-ANI case studies are persuasive demonstrations
that a single ANI scalar can miss functional loci and rearrangements. The exact
controls, negative control, comparator recalibration, and post-hoc audits add
considerable credibility.

The census and broader biological claims require stronger separation of
measurement, assembly and ancestry effects. In particular, `100 - skani ANI` is
an alignable-sequence identity summary rather than a base-level or genome-wide
distance; breakpoint counts are imperfect proxies for rearrangement burden; the
GTDB ANI-structure relationship currently combines a complete-species selection
with an all-pairs quality panel; and the HROM and OAPGC labels remain vulnerable
to shared ancestry and sampling structure. These issues do not undermine the
value of the anchoring concept, but they must be resolved before the census is
presented as a biological decomposition of divergence modes.

### Who would be interested in the results, and why

Method developers and users of genome dereplication, MAG analysis, strain
tracking and pathogen surveillance would be interested in a fast coordinate-aware
alternative that reports shared content and structural descriptors together.
Microbiome researchers would be interested in the catalog-scale observation that
ANI and breakpoint burden are not interchangeable summaries. Public-health and
evolutionary-genomics readers would be interested in the very-high-ANI examples
showing that similar ANI values do not guarantee identical loci or resistance
genotypes.

### Major strengths

1. The core estimand is carefully partitioned. The chain-restricted ANI, aligned
   fraction and structural outputs are distinguished in the simulation, benchmark
   and case-study sections.
2. The exact-truth tests directly probe accessory content, fragmentation,
   indels, GC and rate heterogeneity. The simulations support the claim that the
   estimator is robust to several assembly-level perturbations rather than merely
   showing aggregate correlation.
3. The manuscript reports the limits of its headline gain. Calibrated FastANI is
   more accurate overall, skani is more accurate above 90% ANI, and the low-ANI
   calibrated Syn2bANI value is explicitly described as regression toward the
   band mean rather than discrimination.
4. The biological case studies are effective and generally restrained. The
   abfA locus, cagPAI locality analysis, FDA-ARGOS AMR comparison and the
   structurally conserved *S. aureus* control show when ANI is and is not
   sufficient.
5. QC and revision artifacts are substantive. The GTDB merge audit, dependence
   audit, comparator calibration, circular-origin sensitivity analysis and
   breakpoint-statistic history improve transparency.

### Major Concerns

R2-M1 [measurement-validity]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** Yes
**Claim pointer** The OAPGC analysis interprets `100 - skani ANI` as a sequence-distance axis and infers greater cross-site conspecific sequence divergence.
**Evidence pointer** Online Methods, `Within-species catalog censuses`; Results, `Oral-airway divergence`; `results/revision/DEPENDENCE_AWARE_UNCERTAINTY_REPORT.md`; Supplementary Note 5.
**Concern** The manuscript correctly states that `100 - skani ANI` is ANI-derived and not a counted base-level SNP distance, but the biological language still tends toward genome-wide sequence divergence. ANI summarizes identity among alignable or shared sequence. It can be affected by aligned fraction, assembly contiguity, accessory content and the exclusion of divergent or rearranged material. Syn2bANI's own identifiability analysis shows that unchained divergence is not identifiable and that an oracle restricted to the chained sample still has MAE 1.36 against whole-genome truth. These properties are relevant even though the census ANI layer uses skani, because both classes of methods condition on shared sequence.
**Why it matters** If the alignable fraction or assembly structure differs between oral, airway and within-habitat genomes, an ANI-distance difference may partly reflect what is being compared rather than a greater substitution load. The current N50 sensitivity is helpful but does not define the estimand or jointly control aligned fraction, ancestry and site.
**Resolution test** Define the distance estimand in the census section and replace broad wording such as genome-wide sequence divergence with alignable-sequence ANI distance where necessary. Report the joint distribution of skani ANI, aligned fraction, N50 and completeness for oral-airway and within-habitat pairs, and show that the site effect persists under AF-matched, quality-matched or regression-based sensitivity analyses. Avoid converting ANI-derived distance to SNP load or evolutionary time.

R2-M2 [proxy-validity]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** Yes
**Claim pointer** The HROM and GTDB censuses treat at least two breakpoints and related structural-similarity scores as measures of structural divergence.
**Evidence pointer** Results, `A census separates sequence-divergent and structural-divergent species`; Results, `GTDB extends the census`; Online Methods, `Structural-variant calling`; Results, `Structural outputs agree with independent truth`.
**Concern** The released breakpoint count is much better controlled than the earlier version, but its real-genome agreement remains modest. The paper reports a partial correlation of 0.411 with dnadiff breakpoints after controlling for ANIm and contig count. The method also misses events whose flanking chains have fewer than four anchors, equal-length homologous replacements that keep tags phased, and events hidden by unresolved repeats or contig breaks. A binary cutoff of at least two breakpoints is therefore a detection statistic, not a direct count of all rearrangements.
**Why it matters** Species labeled structurally divergent or breakpoint enriched could differ in detection probability as well as rearrangement biology. This is especially important in multi-species catalogs where genome contiguity, repeat structure and sample composition vary.
**Resolution test** Present breakpoint enrichment as an anchor-derived detection phenotype unless a biological validation is supplied. Report sensitivity to alternative cutoffs such as at least one, at least two and at least three breakpoints, to the continuous count, and to the informative-only denominator. Provide confidence or uncertainty summaries for species-level breakpoint fractions, ideally calibrated to the observable fraction predicted from contiguity.

R2-M3 [observational-confounding]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** Yes
**Claim pointer** The GTDB census supports a catalog-scale relationship between ANI and structural divergence that is not driven by low-quality assemblies.
**Evidence pointer** Results, `GTDB extends the census`; Figure 11; `results/census/gtdb/gtdb_species_ani_sv_joint.tsv`; `results/census/gtdb/effective_quality_sensitivity.tsv`; `results/census/gtdb/GTDB_ANI_MERGE_QC_NOTES.md`.
**Concern** The quality audit is useful, but the ANI-structure panel and the quality panel do not use the same species scope. Figure 11a uses 1,311 complete joint species with at least 100 expected pairs. Large partially covered clusters are excluded from that relationship, including *E. coli* with 338,793,465 expected pairs and structural coverage 0.99521, while Figure 11b describes all effective pairs. In addition, 10.08% of reported GTDB pairs lack a numeric breakpoint. The all-pairs fraction is 77.93%, whereas the breakpoint-informative fraction is 86.66%, but the species-level ANI relationship appears to use the reported denominator. Finally, GTDB species contain related genomes, and pair counts are dominated by a few very large clusters. Species-level summaries reduce pair pseudo-replication but do not remove ancestry, higher-rank phylogenetic structure or cluster dominance.
**Why it matters** The observed decrease in breakpoint-positive species at high ANI may be sensitive to how partial clusters, missing breakpoints and huge species are handled. Without those sensitivity analyses, the catalog-scale relationship is an important observation but not yet an established catalog-wide biological pattern.
**Resolution test** Show the ANI-breakpoint relationship under at least four parallel definitions, namely complete species only, all clusters with high pair coverage, reported-pair denominators and breakpoint-informative denominators. Add weighted and unweighted summaries, leave-one-large-cluster-out checks, and summaries aggregated at genus or family level. State explicitly whether partially covered but near-complete clusters such as *E. coli* change the relationship when analyzed with available-pair denominators. Report uncertainty at the species and higher-taxonomic levels.

R2-M4 [quality-and-ancestry-confounding]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Claim pointer** HROM quality stratification supports the central qualitative conclusion that candidate sequence-level and architecture-level divergence modes can be separated.
**Evidence pointer** Results, `A census separates sequence-divergent and structural-divergent species`; Figure 9; `results/census/hrom/quality_stratified/QUALITY_STRATIFIED_REPORT.md`; Online Methods, `Within-species catalog censuses`.
**Concern** The HROM high-quality restriction is not neutral with respect to the labels. It reduces tested species from 521 to 354 and gives Jaccard overlap of only 0.515 for structural-similarity-enriched species, 0.451 for ANI-similarity-enriched species and 0.196 for species enriched on both axes. SV detection is also strongly associated with minimum pair N50, with 44.0% breakpoint-positive pairs when both genomes have N50 at least 10 kb versus 19.3% otherwise. The species bootstrap quantifies species-composition uncertainty but, as the authors state, does not model pair dependence, shared ancestry or quality correlation.
**Why it matters** The moderate overlap means that the high-quality labels are not simply a pruned version of the all-pair labels. Examples highlighted as biologically interpretable divergence modes may change under quality filters or lineage-resampling. Calling the unfiltered result the central qualitative conclusion risks understating the joint quality and ancestry structure.
**Resolution test** Give the all-pair and high-quality labels equal analytical status and avoid selecting examples that are not robust to the high-quality set. Add a lineage-aware or phylogeny-aware sensitivity analysis, for example resampling within higher-rank groups, adjusting for phylogenetic distance, or holding genome-quality covariates constant. Rename the result as candidate anchor-statistic enrichment unless independent mutation-rate, recombination or rearrangement validation is provided.

R2-M5 [cross-habitat-inference]
**Severity** Major
**Blocking** No
**Claim pointer** Oral-airway conspecifics show greater ANI-derived sequence distance but not a consistent global breakpoint excess.
**Evidence pointer** Results, `Oral-airway divergence`; Figure 10; `results/census/oapgc/site_effects/overall_all_pairs.tsv`; `results/census/oapgc/site_effects/species_effects_all_pairs.tsv`; Online Methods, `Within-species catalog censuses`.
**Concern** The directionality limit and recent-strain power audit are appropriate. Nevertheless, the public site labels are observational and may be mixed, mislabeled or sample-source dependent. Species entering the test need only 30 pairs per group, and the per-species file shows substantial variation in cross-habitat sample size. A median AUC of 0.574 is a small population-level effect, and 143 species have AUC above 0.5 but only 119 remain significant after BH correction. Within-species pair dependence, lineage structure and habitat-associated differences in assembly provenance remain plausible explanations. N50 sensitivity addresses one technical confounder but not ancestry or sampling.
**Why it matters** Without accounting for these correlated design factors, the result supports a label-associated difference in ANI-derived distance more strongly than a habitat-level ecological or dispersal inference.
**Resolution test** Report a prespecified per-species meta-analysis or mixed model with species as the unit and appropriate handling of pair dependence and sample size. Balance or match oral-airway and within-habitat comparisons by N50, completeness, contamination, sequencing/assembly provenance and available lineage indicators. Present the conclusion as an association between public site labels and ANI-derived distance unless stronger metadata and controls support a habitat process.

### Minor Comments

R2-m1 [terminology]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** Use of `hybrid` throughout the census and benchmark sections.
**Evidence pointer** Results, `A census separates sequence-divergent and structural-divergent species`; Online Methods, `Within-species catalog censuses`; Supplementary Note 6.
**Issue** `Hybrid estimator` refers to the raw-or-calibrated Syn2bANI rule, while `hybrid census` refers to a two-tool join of Syn2b structural features and skani ANI.
**Required correction** Use distinct terms such as `raw-or-calibrated estimator` and `two-layer census` to prevent readers from assuming that the census is an end-to-end Syn2bANI validation.

R2-m2 [figure-interpretation]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** Figure 11a and Figure 11b.
**Evidence pointer** Figure 11; `results/census/gtdb/effective_quality_sensitivity.tsv`.
**Issue** Panel a is restricted to complete joint species with at least 100 expected pairs, whereas panel b includes all effective pairs and quality strata. This difference is not prominent enough.
**Required correction** State the different denominators in both the legend and main text, and add a visual or tabular bridge showing how many pairs and species are added by relaxing the complete-cluster requirement.

R2-m3 [biological-claim]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** HROM candidate divergence-mode interpretation.
**Evidence pointer** Figure 9; Results, `A census separates sequence-divergent and structural-divergent species`.
**Issue** The term `mode` may suggest direct mutation, recombination or rearrangement processes even though the test measures enrichment among high ANI or high structural-similarity pairs.
**Required correction** Use `candidate anchor-statistic enrichment phenotype` in the headline and reserve `divergence mode` for independently validated rate measurements.

R2-m4 [metric-labeling]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** OAPGC machine-readable output.
**Evidence pointer** `results/census/oapgc/site_effects/species_effects_all_pairs.tsv`.
**Issue** The column `SNP_auc` represents an AUC based on `100 - skani ANI`, not SNP-level distance.
**Required correction** Rename the column to `ANI_distance_auc` or add a schema note stating that the metric is ANI-derived rather than SNP-derived.

R2-m5 [association-interpretation]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** cagPAI disease-stage and macrolide-resistance analyses.
**Evidence pointer** Results, cagPAI sections; Supplementary Tables S4 and S5.
**Issue** The lineage-stratified analyses are handled well, but locality and resistance tests remain post hoc and structured by lineage.
**Required correction** Keep these analyses clearly labeled as exploratory and avoid any mechanistic disease or resistance inference beyond the reported associations.

R2-m6 [phenotype-vs-genotype]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** FDA-ARGOS *E. coli* resistance comparison.
**Evidence pointer** Results, `Very-high-ANI reference genomes differ in resistance cassettes and structure`; Supplementary Table S7.
**Issue** The analysis compares AMR genotypes and does not establish phenotypic resistance or expression differences.
**Required correction** State that genotype discordance is the measured outcome and that phenotypic susceptibility was not tested in this dataset.

R2-m7 [software-provenance]
**Severity** Minor
**Affected element** Companion Syn2b structural layer.
**Evidence pointer** Online Methods, `Within-species catalog censuses`; Code availability.
**Issue** Syn2bANI, skani and FastANI versions are specified, but the companion Syn2b version or commit used for census structural calls is not stated with comparable precision.
**Required correction** Add the exact Syn2b version, commit, structural-command parameters and anchor-file format used for HROM, OAPGC and GTDB.

### Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established

The blocking issues are R2-M1, R2-M2 and R2-M3. They concern the definition and
biological interpretation of the census distance and structural axes, the
sensitivity of the GTDB catalog-scale relationship to partial clusters and
missing breakpoints, and the need to separate alignable-sequence identity from
genome-wide divergence.

### Assessment against Nature-style criteria

**Originality**
The supplied material supports a plausible methodological advance in combining
fixed-position anchors, chain-restricted ANI estimation and structural
descriptors. External novelty is not assessable from this packet.

**Scientific importance**
The estimator and high-ANI demonstrations are important for dereplication, strain
comparison and surveillance. The catalog-scale biological importance is promising
but not yet established because of the confounding and estimand issues above.

**Interdisciplinary readership**
The topic is relevant to microbiome researchers, public-health genomicists and
genome-engineering users. The manuscript is long and dense, but the explicit
decision rules for raw, calibrated, flagged and hybrid outputs will help applied
readers if the census terminology is tightened.

**Technical soundness**
The core simulations, ANIm benchmark, quality analyses and self-controls are
strong. Technical soundness is weaker for the broad census claims because
breakpoint detection, missing-data handling, quality, ancestry and site labels
are correlated and currently analyzed separately rather than jointly.

**Readability for nonspecialists**
The writing is direct and the boundary statements are commendable. Still,
nonspecialists will need a concise summary that distinguishes ANI among alignable
sequence, aligned fraction, anchor-derived breakpoint detection and biological
rearrangement rate.

### Recommendation posture

Promising and substantially strengthened by the revision materials, but the
census biological case is currently not established from the supplied evidence. I
would be supportive once the estimand, breakpoint-proxy, partial-coverage,
ancestry and cross-habitat concerns are addressed or the corresponding claims are
narrowed.

## Risk / unsupported claims

### Unsupported novelty claims

Not assessable from the supplied material because external methods literature was
outside the review boundary.

### Significance claims not established by the supplied evidence

The manuscript should not yet imply that GTDB, HROM or OAPGC species are
separated into measured mutation, recombination or genome-architecture modes.
The supplied evidence supports candidate enrichment labels and quality-sensitive
screening phenotypes.

### Missing controls, validations, or comparisons

Important missing analyses are AF-matched or quality-matched OAPGC tests,
lineage-aware HROM and OAPGC sensitivity analyses, breakpoint-informative GTDB
species sensitivity, inclusion or exclusion sensitivity for large partial
clusters, and a clear uncertainty analysis for Figure 11a.

### Readability claims that cannot be assessed

Visual legibility of the rendered figures cannot be assessed because image files
were not among the supplied inputs. The figure scripts and legends were reviewed.

### Reliance on partial material

Pair-level HROM and GTDB census records, rendered figures, site metadata and
companion Syn2b provenance were absent. The census concerns are therefore based
on the manuscript, species-level tables, stratum reports, scripts and QC notes
rather than independent recomputation of the full pair-level joins.
